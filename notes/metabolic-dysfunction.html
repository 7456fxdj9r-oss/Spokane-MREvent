Metabolic Dysfunction

What it is, why it matters, and why almost everyone in the room is touched by it.

Bottom line: Only 1 in 8 American adults are metabolically healthy. The other 7 are somewhere on a spectrum of dysfunction that drives diabetes, heart disease, stroke, and the leading causes of preventable death — and it's largely reversible with the right inputs.

The Headline Stats

12.2% of US adults are metabolically healthy
(Araújo 2019, NHANES 2009–2016)
~38% meet criteria for metabolic syndrome
(NHANES 2017–18)
0.5% of adults with obesity are metabolically healthy
(Araújo 2019)

What "Metabolically Healthy" Actually Means

Araújo et al. (2019) defined it as having all five of these in the optimal range — and not being on medication for any of them:

MarkerOptimal threshold
Waist circumference< 102 cm (40 in) men · < 88 cm (35 in) women
Fasting glucose & A1cGlucose < 100 mg/dL · A1c < 5.7%
Blood pressureSystolic < 120 · Diastolic < 80 mmHg
Triglycerides< 150 mg/dL
HDL cholesterol≥ 40 mg/dL men · ≥ 50 mg/dL women

Miss any one of these (or be on a med for it), and you're not metabolically healthy by this definition.

The Connection to Diabetes, Blood Pressure & Cholesterol

The same biology that drives one drives the others. Insulin resistance — cells stop responding to insulin — is the upstream lever:

  • Diabetes & pre-diabetes: insulin resistance → blood sugar rises → A1c climbs.
  • High blood pressure: insulin resistance increases sodium retention and stiffens vessels → BP rises.
  • "High cholesterol": insulin resistance raises triglycerides, lowers HDL, and shifts LDL toward small dense particles — the pattern that actually predicts heart disease.
When someone tells me they have diabetes and high blood pressure and high cholesterol, I don't think "three problems." I think "one problem with three faces."

Who's Most Affected

  • Weight matters, but isn't the whole story: Among adults at a "normal" BMI, only ~1 in 3 are metabolically healthy. Among adults with obesity, it's 1 in 200 (Araújo 2019).
  • It's getting worse: Metabolic syndrome rose from 25.3% in 1988–94 to ~38% in 2017–18 (NHANES).
  • It's hitting younger: the 20–39 age group has shown the largest recent increases.
  • What helps: never smoking, vigorous physical activity, lower BMI, more education — and structured nutrition interventions (see Arterburn 2019 notes).

The Good News — It's Reversible

  • Even 5–10% weight loss dramatically improves blood sugar, BP, triglycerides, and HDL — and often gets people off medication.
  • The Arterburn 2019 trial showed structured programs producing 5–6% loss in 16 weeks, with more than 1 in 4 OPTAVIA participants losing >10%.
  • Walking 30 min/day, prioritizing protein, and lowering refined carbs have all been shown to move these markers in the right direction.
  • The biggest single predictor in the data: support and accountability. Coaching contact correlated more strongly with outcomes than the diet itself.

What to Say On Stage

"If only 1 in 8 American adults is metabolically healthy, then when you raised your hand a minute ago — that wasn't a quirk of this room. That's the country."
"Diabetes, high blood pressure, high cholesterol — these aren't three different diseases. They're three windows looking into the same room. The room is metabolic dysfunction."
"The body that got you into this can get you out. Five to ten percent weight loss is enough to move every single one of these markers — and we have the data to prove it."
